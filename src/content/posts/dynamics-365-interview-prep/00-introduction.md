Most interview preparation for Dynamics 365 is a list: 100 questions, 100 short answers, memorise them all.

Then the real interview starts, and it doesn't look like the list.

> "What's the difference between a plugin and a flow?"
> "Fine. So in your last project, why did you choose a flow?"
> "What happened when it failed?"
> "How did you find out it failed?"

**Interviewers don't ask questions. They ask one question and build the next three on your answer.** The first one checks that you know the concept. The next three check whether you've actually used it, broken it and fixed it.

This series prepares you for that, level by level.

## The five levels

| Level | Who it's for | What the interviewer is checking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Fundamentals** | Freshers, functional consultants, people moving into CRM | Do you understand how Dataverse is built? |
| **2. Developer** | 1–4 years | Can you build it, and do you know why it breaks? |
| **3. Senior** | 4–8 years | Can you integrate it, deploy it and keep it running? |
| **4. Project round** | Everyone above Level 1 | Can you explain *your* project, two levels deep? |
| **5. Extreme** | Leads and architects | Can you reason through a problem nobody has given you the answer to? |

## How every part works

- **The question**, exactly as an interviewer would ask it.
- **A short answer** you can say out loud in 30 seconds.
- **The follow-up** you should expect next, so you're never surprised.
- **The trap**: the answer that sounds right and loses the offer.

Two parts use a different shape, because the round itself is different:

- **The scenarios (Level 3)** give you a problem, not a question. Each one shows how to think, why it happens, and the fix.
- **The project round (Level 4)** groups questions into the chains interviewers follow, with what each chain is checking and how to answer.

Every part ends with a way to **practise with AI** and a short **before-your-interview checklist**.

The examples use the same lending bank as my Real-World Series, so if you've read those articles, every scenario will feel familiar.

## How to use it

1. **Say the answers out loud.** Reading an answer and saying it under pressure are different skills.
2. **Add your own numbers.** Users, records, response times, hours saved. Numbers make an answer believable.
3. **Never claim what you can't explain two levels deeper.** The follow-up questions will find it.

Start with Level 1: **Dataverse and the data model**.
